## 2. Surfaces audited

The audit covered the fresh-user front door, fictional sample, roster browsing, progressive onboarding, preview/acceptance, Home, Search, the four-week calendar, shift detail, actual-day editor, Roster, Calendar, Pay and payslip checking, Employment & Pay Profile, Data & Sync, backup/import, Cloud and encryption, Settings, Changelog, Feedback, Sources, Safe Data Recovery, roster-history/change tools and the PWA update notice.

The principal recurring defects were not architecture defects. They were inconsistent semantic headings, controls that relied on visual proximity instead of programmatic labels, overly verbose card names, missing dynamic-state announcements, small utility targets, unconstrained decorative motion, and advanced Pay detail competing with the headline result.

## 3. Heading and landmark changes

- The existing primary navigation is now paired with a current-page indication.
- Home retains one visually hidden, meaningful H1; Roster, Calendar, More, onboarding, generated-roster and Pay surfaces now expose their visible title as H1.
- Forward-roster week titles are real H3 headings and label their corresponding week sections.
- Main content and primary-navigation landmarks remain intact. Dialog titles continue to be associated through the Phase-1 dialog controller.
- Browser validation confirmed exactly one visible H1 on Roster, Calendar, More and Pay.

## 4. Keyboard results

- Front door, sample, onboarding, Home, Search, day detail, Pay and Data & Sync were completed with keyboard input.
- Primary navigation now exposes `aria-current="page"`.
- Pay's three visual view selectors are a real tab set with Left/Right/Home/End behavior, selection state and labelled tab panels.
- Search exposes expanded/collapsed state and its result list.
- Day-detail and Data & Sync dialogs still enter at a deterministic control, wrap in both directions, close with Escape and restore focus.
- No new keyboard trap was found. The only containment is the intentional topmost-dialog task.

## 5. Accessible-name changes

- Today no longer repeats every visible fact as one enormous name. Its tested name is 113 characters and conveys date, primary duty/start state and the result of activation.
- Each forward-roster day exposes its full date, work/OR/leave/PH state, sign-on or duty where useful, current/actual/month-boundary state where applicable, and the action “View shift details”. Sign-off and duplicated source detail remain in the detail dialog.
- Manual-pattern checkboxes and time fields now name their weekday and purpose in both onboarding and Employment & Pay Profile.
- Dynamic Pay editor duty, time, status, allowance and quantity controls now identify their date and purpose.
- Static actual-day duty search and allowance controls are explicitly named.

## 6. Calendar accessibility

The four-week view remains a simple chronological set of week sections and day buttons rather than an ARIA grid. This avoids inventing a complex two-dimensional keyboard contract. Automated validation proved:

- 28 days occur in chronological order;
- every day includes a full spoken date and explicit state text;
- OR, ALR and PH do not depend on colour;
- one current date uses `aria-current="date"`;
- all four week headings are exposed semantically;
- month boundaries and recorded actuals are included only when relevant;
- day names remain concise and activate the shared read-only detail dialog.

## 7. Large-text and zoom results

Phone validation at 200% root text produced:

- zero horizontal page overflow;
- zero forward-day cell overflow;
- forward-day targets between roughly 146 and 165 CSS px high after reflow;
- Data & Sync dialog width 370 px within a 390 px viewport;
- a 44 px close control;
- usable internal dialog scrolling.

No text was shrunk to make it fit. Content reflows vertically. Existing Phase-6 WebKit overlay, keyboard, safe-area and VisualViewport rules remain unchanged.

## 8. Touch-target results

- Shared close buttons, search clear, calendar days, history arrows, leave removal, disclosure summaries, week menus and update actions now have a deterministic 44 px minimum hit area where practical.
- Existing primary mobile navigation remains approximately 56 px high.
- Small visual glyphs remain visually compact; the interactive area, not the artwork, is enlarged.

## 9. Colour and contrast findings

- Work, OR, ALR, PH, leave, actual, current-day, sync and error states retain established theme colours but also expose text, borders, markers and accessible names.
- PH keeps a distinct border treatment; actuals retain a textual `ACTUAL` marker; OR and ALR retain literal labels.
- Error text now has a separate high-contrast light-mode value instead of reusing the pale dark-surface colour.
- Visible focus is a 3 px accent outline across interactive elements. Terminal theme retains its deliberate high-contrast dashed white treatment.
- Theme architecture and accepted state colours were not redesigned.

## 10. Reduced-motion work

A `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` contract collapses decorative animation and transition duration and disables smooth scrolling. Navigation, Data & Sync, Calendar flow and Employment & Pay Profile scrolling use feature-detected reduced-motion behavior. Existing meaningful status changes remain visible. Chromium confirmed the media query, `auto` scroll behavior, and effectively zero transition/animation duration.

## 11. Dynamic announcements

- Search reports a restrained result count or no-match result and clears the status when the query clears.
- Update readiness is explicitly a polite status.
- Data, Cloud, encryption, feedback and actual-duty lookup retain targeted polite status regions.
- Setup, Employment Profile and payslip validation failures use alert semantics and focus the affected control.
- Live regions were intentionally limited; ordinary re-rendering is not announced.

## 12. Form and error improvements

- Every audited Pay setup selector has an associated label and relevant helper association.
- Previous/next pay-period glyph buttons have meaningful names.
- Onboarding validation now places the explanation in the setup surface and focuses the first unresolved choice/field rather than relying only on a blocking alert.
- Employment role validation and payslip-gross validation identify the invalid control, associate the error and move focus to it.
- Dynamic and static day-editor inputs no longer rely on placeholder or row proximity for their name.

## 13. Pay cognitive-load changes

Expected gross remains the first summary and is now visually dominant across two summary columns (the full row on phone), with a larger accent amount. Detailed additional-pay, PH and rounding evidence remains available beside/below it. Pay's view switcher now behaves as an accessible tab task. Calculation rules and audit evidence were not removed or changed.

## 14. Calendar cognitive-load changes

The accepted distinction remains unchanged and clear: “Keep my calendar updated automatically” versus a one-off export. Phase 6 added reduced-motion-safe progressive movement and preserved the existing privacy explanation, sharing fallback and backend security contract.

## 15. Recovery accessibility

Safe Data Recovery already had the correct architecture and was preserved. It provides a single H1, saved-copy comparison sections, a status region, clear action separation, explicit destructive confirmation, export-before-replace language and a collapsed Technical details section. It remains fail-closed and cannot be dismissed as an ordinary warning.

## 21. Known remaining accessibility issues

- Real VoiceOver quality remains a human beta gate.
- Very high text enlargement beyond the tested 200% can make the 28-day visual calendar intentionally tall. It remains chronological and operable, but a future optional list presentation may be worth user research rather than being added speculatively.
- Some legacy success/confirmation paths still use native `alert()` or `confirm()`. They remain keyboard/screen-reader operable, but consolidating non-destructive confirmations into the application's status pattern is later polish.
- Comprehensive contrast measurement on physical iOS display modes and user-selected colour filters remains a manual task; no state relies on colour alone.
- This phase did not attempt a full screen-reader emulation of third-party browser chrome, native file pickers or OS share sheets.

## 22. Recommendation for Phase 7

Proceed to Phase 7 only after the must-pass human beta checks below succeed. The implementation has a coherent semantic hierarchy, labelled forms, consistent focus behavior, accessible calendar/day navigation, useful large-text reflow and reduced-motion support without changing schema-5, Cloud, encryption, PWA update or product information architecture.

## Human beta checklist

### Must pass before Phase 6 acceptance

1. With keyboard only, move through front door → sample → setup; then configured Home → Search → a roster day → close. Focus must always be visible and return sensibly.
2. At 200% browser text/zoom on phone and desktop, inspect Home, four-week roster, day editor, Pay and Data & Sync. No control may be clipped or require horizontal page scrolling.
3. On iPhone VoiceOver, read Today, then swipe through one calendar week. Day names should be concise, chronological and explicit about work/OR/ALR/PH.
4. On iPad VoiceOver, open and close shift detail, actual-day editor and Data & Sync. Focus must enter the task, stay contained and return to the invoker.
5. Trigger an onboarding validation error and an empty payslip check. VoiceOver must announce the error and focus the affected choice/field.
6. In Pay, identify expected gross quickly and switch the three Pay views with VoiceOver or a hardware keyboard.
7. Enable Reduce Motion, move among Home/Roster/Calendar/More and open Data & Sync. No decorative sliding/smooth movement should remain.
8. With a synthetic recovery fixture only, confirm the recovery heading, saved-copy summaries, evidence action and destructive confirmation are understandable and keyboard reachable.

### Nice-to-have polish

- Note any VoiceOver phrase that feels longer than needed even though it is correct.
- Compare every optional theme with colour filters and report combinations that feel low contrast.
- Try 300–400% desktop zoom and record surfaces that become cumbersome while remaining technically usable.

## Accepted Phase-6 refinement — Example Roster visual continuity

The fictional Example Roster now uses the same narrow entry-surface system as the accepted front door instead of maintaining a second legacy presentation. The deliberately small shared vocabulary is:

- `v32-entry-surface` for width, outer rhythm and responsive containment;
- `v32-entry-hero` for the bordered gradient introduction, typography and phone treatment;
- `v32-entry-actions` for primary/secondary action sizing and mobile stacking;
- `v32-entry-stack` for section rhythm;
- `v32-entry-card` and `v32-entry-card--accent` for ordinary and highlighted card shells.

Demo-only roster cells, pay evidence, example-duty dialog and fictional-state behavior remain deliberately specialised. The duplicated demo width, heading scale, hero spacing, card background/border/radius and phone action rules were removed. The original front-door declarations were moved into the shared primitives without changing their computed phone appearance.

Browser evidence covers 390 × 844, 768 × 1024 and 1440 × 900 layouts; all seven themes in dark and light modes; 200% text; keyboard/dialog behavior; Back/Forward/reload/offline reopen; and zero personal storage or generation change. The demo remains one interaction from the front door, with generation `1 → 1`, zero localStorage/IndexedDB personal mutations, and zero Cloud or production contacts.
