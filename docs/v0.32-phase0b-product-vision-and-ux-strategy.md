# RosterBot v0.32 Phase 0B — Product Vision, Source Diagnosis & Future-State UX Strategy

Status: audit and planning only. This report is based on the frozen blind audit in `docs/v0.32-phase0a-blind-adoption-audit.md`, the released v0.31.0 source at `41e09a2c7c38654e51cbf4c5bdf05c8e72044a02`, and the stated long-term roster-source hierarchy. It does not modify or reinterpret the Phase 0A evidence, change application behaviour, or specify the private ingestion/security implementation.

## 1. Executive strategic verdict

RosterBot should stop presenting itself primarily as a roster generator that a driver must configure and start presenting itself as the driver's trustworthy answer to three questions:

> What am I working, what changed, and what should I be paid?

The v0.31 configured Home already demonstrates the right product centre: Today, the current week/fortnight, upcoming work and rest, and expected pay. The future published-roster ambition strengthens that conclusion. A mature RosterBot should open on the best currently known answer, use the master roster only where no more authoritative assignment exists, identify meaningful changes, and let the driver record what actually happened.

The long-term source model is not “replace the roster engine.” It is a layered authority model:

1. master-roster projection;
2. weekly published assignment;
3. daily published assignment;
4. driver-recorded actual work.

Each later layer overrides the relevant displayed duty without destroying the earlier evidence. This makes the existing master-roster engine, dated roster history, day overrides, diary, PayBot, offline shell, schema-5 persistence and Cloud safety valuable foundations. It also means much of today's first-run setup is temporary acquisition machinery, not a durable product mental model.

v0.32 should therefore do three things in order:

1. remove the two trust-breaking technical hazards exposed by Phase 0A: the canonical-write close race and incomplete modal focus containment;
2. make first value precede advanced configuration, using today's manual engine without pretending its complete setup wizard is the future front door;
3. align Home, terminology and trust messaging with the future “best known roster + changes + actual + pay” model, while retaining manual setup as an explicit fallback.

It would be wasteful to lavish polish on the current eight-decision form as though every future user will always need to identify a projection anchor, schedule mode, depot, roster, line and alternate line manually. Improve its clarity and safety as a fallback; do not make it the permanent centre of the product.

## 2. How the long-term vision changes the interpretation of Phase 0A

Phase 0A found that users are asked to understand RosterBot's model before RosterBot proves its value. Source inspection confirms that the form is not merely verbose UI: it constructs the inputs required to synthesize a master-roster projection and an effective-dated employment record. The form's density mirrors the current data acquisition architecture.

The future vision changes the strategic meaning of that friction:

- **Starting date, depot, roster, line and alternation are presently inputs to projection.** With authorised weekly/daily assignment ingestion and verified identity, they should usually arrive as facts or be inferred from dated official data. They remain useful for long-range projection and fallback, but should no longer define first use.
- **Role and employment history remain important to pay.** They are more durable than roster-generator inputs, but they should be confirmed when the user asks for a trustworthy pay estimate rather than demanded before the product shows any roster value. Future employer-authorised data could prefill them, but the user must be able to verify and correct them.
- **The preview is not obsolete.** “Does this look like your roster?” is an excellent confidence mechanism. In a future product it becomes confirmation of imported/inferred identity and assignments, not necessarily confirmation of a manually configured generator.
- **The diary is not an override implementation detail.** It is the highest-authority user record: the distinction between what was projected, what was published and what was actually worked is central to the product.
- **Home becomes more valuable, not less.** Automatic roster sources remove setup work and let the existing strong dashboard become the first experience rather than the reward after setup.
- **Change-awareness becomes possible.** Retaining prior sources allows RosterBot to say that tomorrow's assignment changed, which may be more compelling than merely showing the latest shift.
- **Trust must move earlier.** Future identity and operational data increase the need for a simple explanation of source, device storage, sync and access. They do not justify exposing schemas, tokens, attestation or database-provider vocabulary as the primary explanation.

The Phase 0A verdict is therefore not “make the current wizard prettier.” It is “move today's wizard out of the critical path wherever possible, and make its remaining use progressive, explicit and safe.”

## 3. A/B/C/D classification of current onboarding information

The classifications below describe the mature product's normal path. Some items retain a different classification when used as manual fallback.

| Current information or task | Class | Why | v0.32 implication |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Starting date / roster anchor | **B — necessary today, ultimately automatable** | The current engine needs a dated point on a master rotation. Published assignment history or a verified roster association should normally supply the usable period and projection anchor. | Ask only in manual setup. Explain it as “the week this roster position applied,” not a universal account fact. |
| Role / pay classification | **A — permanently necessary user knowledge or confirmation** | Pay cannot be responsibly estimated without the applicable classification. An authorised source may prefill it, but the driver must be able to confirm/correct it. | Postpone until pay is introduced. Use a familiar role label first; retain payroll codes as supporting detail. |
| Role-effective date | **B — necessary today, ultimately automatable** | Dated classification history is required for accurate historic pay, but payroll/employment data could supply it. Many users will not know why it is needed before seeing value. | Do not block the first roster view. Ask when enabling historical/accurate pay or correcting an inferred record. |
| Scheduling mode | **D — architectural dead end in the normal path** | “Official/master,” “regular manual pattern” and “no default pattern” select an internal generation strategy. A mature product should know what authoritative data is available and offer manual entry only when it is not. | Keep the choice only in an advanced manual fallback path. Do not teach it as a core RosterBot concept. |
| Depot | **B** | It is currently required to select the correct frozen roster set. Verified identity or published assignment should normally provide it, with confirmation only when ambiguous. | Keep editable in manual setup and profile correction; do not demand it before demo/first value. |
| Roster | **B** | The master rotation is a useful projection source, but a user's current assignment should normally be supplied or inferred. | Retain as a projection/fallback setting and provenance detail. |
| Line | **B** | It anchors the current rotation today. Weekly/daily assignments should make the current duty authoritative without requiring users to maintain line arithmetic. | Retain as manual projection input and optional “how projected” detail. |
| Alternate roster/line | **B**, becoming **C** as a manual exception | The current generator needs the second track for alternating arrangements. Published assignments can make most of this invisible; some users will still need a fallback projection. | Hide unless a user explicitly chooses manual alternating setup. Do not show in default onboarding. |
| Manual work pattern / no default pattern | **C — advanced or optional** | It correctly supports trainees and roles not represented by master rotations and remains essential as a fallback when authoritative data is unavailable. It is not the normal first-use concept. | Preserve and improve discoverability for affected users, outside the minimum path. |
| Annual leave during first setup | **C** | Useful for immediate accuracy, but it can be added later and future operational feeds may reflect some leave/assignment effects. | Remove from minimum setup; offer after the first useful roster appears. |
| Roster Lookup, as currently implemented | **D** | It claims to be low-commitment exploration but reuses nearly the complete personal setup form, including pay-related fields unrelated to browsing a roster. | Replace the current product role; see section 10. Preserve the underlying official-roster browser capability. |
| Preview / roster confirmation | **A** | A driver must be able to verify that RosterBot has associated the right roster or imported the right assignment. This remains important even when data is automatic. | Preserve the preview-before-authority principle; adapt it to inferred/imported data and exceptions. |
| Employment & Pay Profile | **A** for current and dated employment facts; **C** for advanced history administration | Pay accuracy permanently depends on employment facts, while manually managing a whole dated event timeline is a power-user/recovery task. | Introduce the minimum role confirmation at the point of pay value. Keep full dated history in an advanced profile. |
| Account / restore | **C today**, with a future **B** identity dependency | v0.31 works locally without an account. Future private published-roster delivery may require verified identity, but identity should be requested with a clear benefit and is not synonymous with Cloud Sync. | Keep restore visible for returning users without making it part of the new-user form. In future distinguish “verify who I am” from “sync my devices.” |
| Cloud Sync | **C** | Cross-device continuity is valuable but ordinary local use must remain possible. Future ingestion may require a service connection, but users need not understand Cloud mechanics to see their roster. | Offer after local value or when the user asks to use another device. Keep local-first operation. |
| Encryption/passphrase/recovery details | **C** | Important security controls for users who enable encrypted sync; irrelevant before that choice. | Reveal only within the explicit encrypted-sync path, with technical evidence available on demand. |
| Calendar | **C** | A strong adoption/retention feature, but it depends on having useful roster data and should not obstruct first value. | Prompt after the user has verified the roster and can understand what will be added. |
| Actual-day editing / diary | **A** | It records the highest-authority outcome: what the driver actually worked. No published feed can safely infer every operational change after publication. | Retain as a central “correct/record this day” action, not a hidden implementation of day overrides. |
| Payslip check and allowances | **C**, built on **A** employment facts | Powerful after the user trusts roster/pay output, but unnecessary for first roster value. | Progressively disclose from the expected-pay card and the relevant day/pay cycle. |
| Backup/export/import | **C** | Essential resilience and portability, not part of seeing first value. | Prompt after meaningful local data exists; explain it as “keep/move my RosterBot,” not a storage implementation. |

The durable user knowledge is smaller than today's form suggests: who the driver is (when needed), whether the displayed work is theirs, the employment facts needed for pay, and what they actually worked. Most rotation mechanics are temporary inputs to today's projection engine.

## 4. Current workflows that should survive long term

### Home as the recurring starting point

The configured dashboard already answers the highest-frequency questions with little interaction. Source inspection shows it derives Today, the week strip, next work, current-fortnight expected pay and payslip status from the existing roster/diary/pay engines (`js/70-navigation.js:108-116`). This composition should survive even when the winning duty comes from a weekly or daily feed rather than a master projection.

### Preview and explicit confirmation

The current preview lets a driver compare generated dates, duties and times before creating their personal timeline (`index.html:380-384`). The durable principle is visible evidence before accepting an association or replacement. Future automatic data should strengthen, not remove, this confidence step where identity or roster matching is uncertain.

### Long-range master-roster projection

The effective-dated timeline and frozen roster sets remain valuable beyond the published weekly/daily horizon. The engine already chooses a dated arrangement and calculates a long-range rotation (`js/11-roster-engine.js:108-137`). That should remain the fallback source labelled “Projected,” not be discarded when richer sources arrive.

### Actual worked diary and manual correction

The existing day record retains an actual duty, times, status, notes, allowances and a reference to what was originally rostered (`js/60-diary.js:199`; `js/42-paybot.js:90-96`). This is conceptually aligned with the future hierarchy. Automatic daily assignments must never overwrite an established actual record.

### Expected pay and payslip checking

The connection between roster/actual work, expected pay and a later payslip comparison is one of RosterBot's strongest differentiators. Keep the detailed engine and auditability; improve when and how its complexity is revealed.

### Offline/local-first use

Local operation, schema-5 transactional persistence, backups, optional encrypted Cloud and the v0.31 offline shell are strategic, not transitional. Train drivers need the product where reception is unreliable. Future ingestion adds a remote source but must not turn ordinary viewing/editing into an online-only experience.

### Calendar, source inspection and history

Calendar output, official roster browsing, source references, dated employment history and retained prior roster arrangements remain valuable secondary tools. They should be reached by user intent rather than presented as prerequisites.

## 5. Current workflows that should become fallback or advanced

- **Full manual master-roster setup:** retain it for unsupported users, outages, historical reconstruction and cases without organisational cooperation. Name it as manual setup, not simply “setup.”
- **Roster/line/depot changes and alternation arithmetic:** keep as an advanced projection editor. Future published assignments should override the affected period without rewriting the master baseline.
- **Manual patterns and ad-hoc fortnight entry:** preserve as first-class fallbacks for trainees, DTCOs, RDSs and anyone outside supplied rotations, but branch into them only when relevant.
- **Full employment-history administration:** preserve for corrections, progression and historical pay. Ordinary users should normally confirm a current role and only add effective-date detail when it changes a calculation.
- **Official roster-book browsing:** preserve as a reference tool separate from personal onboarding.
- **Backup, import, Cloud, encryption migration and conflict tools:** preserve their safety and explicit consent, but organise them under recognisable outcomes such as backing up, moving to another phone and resolving a sync issue.
- **Calendar publishing options:** keep the detailed choices after a simple “put my roster in my calendar” entry point.

Manual fallback must remain capable rather than second-rate. A user without access to future ingestion should still be able to construct the same canonical personal state, use the app offline and correct every day. The strategic change is where the fallback sits, not whether it exists.

## 6. Current concepts likely to disappear from normal use

The following should not become long-term concepts every driver must learn:

- choosing a scheduling engine mode before seeing anything useful;
- choosing a projection anchor date when authoritative assignments already exist;
- maintaining roster-line progression and alternate-line parity as ordinary housekeeping;
- the legacy distinction between “Quick / Basic” and “Personal Diary” as a user-facing model;
- entering role-effective history merely to get through roster onboarding;
- understanding the difference between session settings, timeline, week overrides and day overrides;
- interpreting “Roster Lookup” as a second copy of personal setup;
- understanding Supabase, schema numbers, capability attestation, revisions, tokens or migration wrappers to decide whether sync is safe;
- treating PayBot as a separate product boundary rather than RosterBot's pay capability.

These may continue to exist internally or in diagnostics. Their disappearance from normal use does not imply deleting working engines or safety evidence.

## 7. Proposed future user mental model

The primary object should be **My work**, not a configured generator. For each date RosterBot presents the best known duty and, only when useful, its source.

| User-facing state | Meaning | Normal presentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Projected** | Calculated from the master rotation because no later official assignment is available. | A subtle label on future duties; explanation on tap. |
| **Weekly roster** | The driver was assigned a shift in the published weekly wallsheet. | Usually implicit; label when it differs from projection or provenance is requested. |
| **Daily roster** | The latest official pre-work assignment/full duty is available. | “Daily roster confirmed” near the relevant duty, especially for tomorrow/today. |
| **Changed** | A newer source materially differs from what RosterBot previously showed. This is an event, not a separate authority level. | A concise change callout containing old and new duty/time and when it was learned. |
| **My correction** | The driver has deliberately corrected an assignment before recording the final actual. | Clearly user-entered; never silently represented as official. |
| **Actual** | What the driver ultimately worked or recorded as reality. | Visually authoritative for diary and pay; published/projection detail remains inspectable. |

Most users should not see a five-layer stack on every day. They should see one clear answer. Provenance becomes prominent when it affects confidence: a duty is only projected, an assignment changed, the official data is stale/unavailable, or actual work differs. A detail view can reveal the complete chain.

Three invariants should guide later design:

1. A later official source may replace the displayed assignment for its period but never destroy the baseline or earlier evidence.
2. Official daily publication is not proof of actual work and cannot overwrite a driver-confirmed actual.
3. Unknown information remains unknown; RosterBot must not make a projection look officially confirmed.

The current engine already contains useful seeds: a dated baseline timeline, week-level overrides, day/actual overrides and “Originally rostered” display. It does not yet model weekly and daily publications as separate provenance-bearing sources. Adding that model is future product/data work, not a v0.32 UI shortcut.

## 8. Home/dashboard strategic direction

Home should remain the durable centre of RosterBot. Automatic ingestion should make it arrive sooner and become more trustworthy, not replace it with an inbox or setup hub.

The first viewport should prioritise:

1. **Today:** current best-known duty, sign-on/off, location/detail as useful, and a small confidence/source status.
2. **Tomorrow:** enough information to plan, with a prominent but calm “changed” treatment when applicable.
3. **Next rest:** the next useful run of days off, not merely a requirement to decode `OR` in a grid.
4. **Current week/fortnight:** a compact visual overview and direct route to detail.
5. **Expected pay:** amount, period and whether it is based on projected, published or actual/recorded information.

Progressively disclose:

- the complete fortnight/month;
- source history and superseded assignments;
- detailed pay components and rate evidence;
- employment history;
- roster-line/projector mechanics;
- sync and calendar configuration.

The default experience should answer “what do I need to know now?” before offering “how did RosterBot calculate this?” The existing Home search should not outrank Today, Tomorrow or a material roster change.

The central promise should be:

> Know what you're working, what changed, and what you should be paid — even offline.

## 9. Published-roster provenance and change UX

Change-awareness could become RosterBot's strongest necessity-maker because the cost of missing a changed duty is immediate and personal. A static master-roster viewer competes with existing documents; a tool that notices the weekly or daily assignment changed creates a reason to open it every day.

Product-level requirements for later work:

- Compare each newer authoritative assignment with the prior winning assignment for the same date.
- Distinguish “new confirmation” from a meaningful change. A daily roster matching the weekly roster should reassure, not alarm.
- Describe changes in driver language: “Tomorrow changed from SP123 at 08:00 to SP456 at 11:00.”
- Retain enough earlier evidence to explain the comparison and avoid a latest-value-only black box.
- Show source and publication time when the user inspects the change.
- Let the driver record a further operational change or actual work without mislabelling it as official.
- Avoid notification design until identity, authorisation, source timeliness and false-positive rules are proven.

Provenance should also qualify pay. “Expected pay from your published roster” and “Updated from actual entries” are more meaningful than raw confidence derived from internal flags.

## 10. Roster Lookup strategic recommendation

The current Roster Lookup should not survive unchanged. `showRosterLookup()` reuses the onboarding surface and merely changes its title/action while suppressing persistence (`js/70-navigation.js:38-57`). That is technically economical but breaks the user's expectation of a lightweight look.

Split its jobs:

1. **Try RosterBot / sample experience:** a believable, explicitly fictional example showing Today, a fortnight and expected pay with no personal questions and no saved personal state. Its purpose is adoption, not configuration.
2. **Browse master rosters & shifts:** the existing official-roster viewer for drivers who want to inspect a depot, roster, line or duty. It should not ask for role/pay classification or role-effective date.
3. **Set up manually:** the current projection setup, progressively presented and clearly identified as the fallback path for making the roster personal.
4. **Future verified roster:** identity-driven access to the user's published assignment, once the private ingestion product exists.

The label “Roster Lookup” is suitable for the second job, not all four. Do not force it to be both a demo and a personal setup mode.

## 11. Trust and privacy UX direction

The source confirms that RosterBot has strong safety properties, but Data & Sync presents many of them in implementation language. The primary trust explanation should answer user questions directly.

### What an ordinary user should be able to learn quickly

- **What RosterBot has:** “Your roster, diary and pay settings,” with a plain summary of the current sources.
- **Where it is:** “The working copy is saved on this device.”
- **Whether it is synced:** “Not synced,” “up to date,” “offline changes on this device,” or “needs attention.”
- **Who can access it:** a simple account/encryption answer appropriate to the selected mode, without overclaiming what future operational-source administrators may access.
- **Why sign-in or identity is needed:** “to restore on another device” today; potentially “to show only your authorised published roster” in the future.
- **What happens on a new phone:** restore from encrypted sync or import a backup; neither should be described through schemas or revisions.
- **Where a duty came from:** projected master roster, weekly roster, daily roster, user correction or actual record.

### What remains available as technical transparency

Provider, encryption design, capability/contract status, revision/conflict evidence, schema versions, deployment identity and privacy details should remain accessible in an expandable technical section or diagnostics export. Hiding them from the primary decision is not hiding them from scrutiny.

### Future identity boundary

“Account,” “verified employee identity,” “device sync” and “encrypted Cloud storage” are different user propositions and must not be collapsed into one Cloud modal. Employer-approved identity may eventually unlock operational roster assignments without necessarily granting an employer access to the driver's private actual notes or pay diary. That separation must be explicit in product promises before implementation.

Open-source application code should continue to be described separately from private operational data. Public code does not justify exposing multi-employee wallsheets or daily rosters to browsers, repositories or shared caches.

## 12. Safe Data Recovery root-cause diagnosis

### Finding

The immediate-close failure is real, reproducible outside the Phase 0A harness and represents a genuine transactional boundary defect. The recovery system is correctly refusing to accept an inconsistent committed envelope/projection, but the application creates that inconsistency during ordinary first-run interaction.

In controlled fresh-profile tests at the released v0.31.0 source:

- changing only `startLine` and closing at 0 ms or 25 ms reopened into `unrecoverable` recovery with the reason “Committed schema 5 or its compatibility evidence does not verify”;
- waiting 75 ms, 200 ms, 750 ms or 2.5 seconds before closing reopened normally;
- the failed cases retained the prior generation-1 empty schema-5 envelope while `rosterbot-session-settings-v1` already contained the new canonical personal value;
- localStorage and IndexedDB therefore described a compatibility projection different from the committed envelope.

### Exact write path

1. Every onboarding field change is bound to `saveRosterSession()` (`js/20-roster-ui.js:1084`).
2. `saveRosterSession()` writes canonical `rosterbot-session-settings-v1` directly to localStorage and then calls `notifyPersonalChange()` (`js/20-roster-ui.js:273-275`).
3. The registry classifies the whole session-settings object as canonical personal state, includes it in Cloud and IndexedDB surfaces (`js/01-persistence-registry.js:23-35, 158-171`), and schema 5 fingerprints it.
4. `notifyPersonalChange()` does not synchronously commit the envelope. It appends a 40 ms delayed projection sync to `projectionSync` (`js/03-persistence-bootstrap.js:26-43`).
5. If the page is closed during that window, the timer/transaction does not finish. The diary's `visibilitychange` and `pagehide` handlers independently call `mirrorNow()` (`js/60-diary.js:216`), which can mirror the new compatibility key but does not turn it into a verified schema-5 generation.
6. On restart, schema-5 validation sees a verified old envelope whose declared projection fingerprint no longer matches the now-present session setting. It fails closed into Safe Data Recovery.

The integrity detector is not inventing damage and should not be weakened. The application has made a canonical value visible before its corresponding generation transaction is durable.

### Confirmation symptom

The confirmation path has the same missing transaction/success boundary at a larger scale. `saveCurrentAsMyRoster()` synchronously returns `true` after direct writes to the timeline, employment profile, annual leave and experience/session state, calls `mirrorNow()` without awaiting it, and does not await `RosterBotSchema5Persistence` (`js/20-roster-ui.js:154-170`). Navigation declares success 80 ms later (`js/70-navigation.js:147`). Several canonical notifications are queued independently, so one logical “Use this as my roster” action can create more than one schema-5 generation.

The exact Phase 0A post-confirmation overlay is timing-sensitive. Six clean controlled reruns with preview dwell times from 0 to 3 seconds did not reproduce the overlay; they did show the confirmation action settling asynchronously and advancing separate session/timeline generations. The frozen two black-box observations remain valid evidence. Their “recovery now, normal after reopen” pattern is consistent with an intermediate verification failure followed by a later queued sync repairing the stored copies while the already-rendered recovery overlay remains. That specific interleaving was not deterministically re-captured, so it should not be reported as proven solely from the rerun.

### Risk and severity

- **Immediate close/background after a canonical field change:** proven production-reachable defect. Mobile users routinely background or kill a PWA immediately. Severity is **high data-integrity/adoption**, with **critical first-run trust impact**.
- **Clean confirmation overlay:** credible timing defect with a proven unsafe success contract, but intermittent. Severity remains **release-blocking for a redesigned onboarding path until the boundary is fixed and stressed**.
- **Existing v0.31 users:** exposed whenever a canonical UI handler writes a projection key directly and the page terminates before the delayed schema-5 commit. This is not limited to first install.

### Recommended remediation scope (not implemented here)

Create one canonical persistence operation for a logical onboarding change/acceptance: stage the new values, transactionally commit/verify the schema-5 envelope and both projections, and only then report success or navigate. Coalesce the confirmation into one intentional generation. Page lifecycle handling should quiesce an already-started commit where the browser permits, but correctness must not depend on an unload callback. Ordinary draft UI values that are intentionally resumable need an explicit classification/transaction policy rather than being written as committed canonical state and reconciled later. Add browser tests for close/background at every transaction boundary, confirmation under delayed IndexedDB, one-generation acceptance, and restart without recovery. Do not suppress recovery or accept a mismatched envelope.

Current persistence tests extensively cover migration, replacement, rollback and recovery decisions, but no test exercises `v28UseRosterBtn`, `saveCurrentAsMyRoster()` or a page close between direct onboarding writes and delayed schema-5 commit.

## 13. Data & Sync focus/modal diagnosis

### Root cause

The markup correctly declares the Data & Sync shell as `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"` and labels it with `dataTitle` (`index.html:880-883`). The JavaScript implements only visual state:

- `show()` removes `hidden`, adds the body scroll-lock class and refreshes status;
- `hide()` restores `hidden` and removes the class;
- Escape, backdrop and Close dismiss the panel (`js/50-data-backup.js:35-38, 84`).

It does not:

- capture the invoking element;
- move focus into the dialog on ordinary open;
- constrain Tab/Shift+Tab to visible dialog controls;
- make the background inert or remove it from the accessibility tree;
- restore focus to the invoker on close.

`showCloud()` can focus a Cloud field for a specific flow, but that does not repair ordinary Data & Sync opening. Other modal helpers are similarly visual (`js/70-navigation.js:120-121`), and modal behavior is implemented independently across feature files.

The Phase 0A sequence is therefore expected: focus stays on the background Data & Sync card, then Tab reaches background Settings, Changelog and Feedback before finally entering the dialog. `aria-modal` describes intended semantics; it does not implement focus behavior.

### Required accessibility remediation

Introduce a reusable dialog controller, rather than a one-off Data & Sync patch, that:

1. captures the invoker;
2. focuses the dialog's heading/close button or first task control on open;
3. traps forward and reverse keyboard traversal within the active topmost dialog;
4. marks the rest of the application inert, with a reviewed fallback for browsers lacking `inert`;
5. closes predictably on Escape where safe;
6. restores focus to the original invoker, or a deterministic nearby control if it no longer exists;
7. handles dynamically revealed Cloud/encryption controls and nested confirmation tasks;
8. does not disturb the Phase 6 keyboard/VisualViewport geometry fixes.

Test mouse/touch open, keyboard open/close, first and last tabbable controls, Shift+Tab, Escape, backdrop, focus restoration, dynamically hidden controls, and every shared modal. This is an accessibility and task-integrity defect, not cosmetic polish.

## 14. Proposed v0.32 implementation phases in priority order

### Phase 1 — Persistence and modal trust blockers

- Make onboarding draft/acceptance writes transactionally coherent with schema 5.
- Ensure one logical confirmation creates one intended generation.
- Add termination/background fault tests around the direct-write/delayed-sync boundary.
- Establish a reusable accessible dialog/focus-containment contract and apply it to Data & Sync first, then shared overlays.

Exit criterion: no ordinary first-run/background sequence enters recovery, and modal tasks are keyboard-contained without persistence changes caused by focus.

### Phase 2 — Value-first product framing and genuine exploration

- Rewrite the first 30 seconds around Today, changes, days off, expected pay and offline use.
- Provide an explicitly sample/demo experience with no personal setup or persistence.
- Separate official master-roster browsing from personal setup.
- Put the concise local-first/account-optional trust promise beside the first personal question.

Exit criterion: a reluctant user can understand the complete benefit and see a realistic result before committing personal data.

### Phase 3 — Progressive manual onboarding

- Define the minimum manual inputs needed to show a credible personal roster today.
- Move role history, annual leave, alternation and manual-pattern detail to contextual steps.
- State why each answer is needed, whether a default is a suggestion and how to change it later.
- Preserve preview-before-save and all fallback capabilities.

Exit criterion: first roster value requires materially fewer visible decisions, while every current supported arrangement remains reachable.

### Phase 4 — Home and terminology alignment

- Make Today/Tomorrow/next rest/current fortnight/expected pay the explicit hierarchy.
- Clarify projected, rostered/published, user-corrected and actual information using current data honestly.
- Remove misleading “recorded” language where data is still roster-derived.
- Reorganise secondary entry points around jobs: roster changed, role changed, payslip arrived, back up, move device.

Exit criterion: returning users can answer core work/pay questions without learning implementation categories.

### Phase 5 — Trust, identity and data-source presentation

- Restructure the primary Data & Sync copy into device, backup and other-device outcomes.
- Keep protocol/security evidence in progressive technical details.
- Prototype source summaries that can later accommodate master, weekly, daily, manual and actual provenance.
- Define future-facing language that separates identity verification from optional device sync without promising unavailable ingestion.

Exit criterion: users can explain where their data is, what is synced, who can access it and why identity would be requested.

### Phase 6 — Accessibility and cognitive-load pass

- Complete heading hierarchy, concise accessible names, errors/instructions association, large-text resilience and keyboard paths.
- Audit every modal against the reusable controller.
- Test screen-reader landmarks, focus restoration and reduced-motion/mobile behavior.

Exit criterion: setup and core recurring tasks are operable and understandable without sight, mouse precision or implementation knowledge.

### Phase 7 — Source/provenance UX research, not ingestion implementation

- Use synthetic local fixtures to test how drivers understand “Projected,” “Weekly roster,” “Daily roster,” “Changed” and “Actual.”
- Test whether change callouts help without creating alarm fatigue.
- Define the user-visible contract and acceptance language before choosing backend schemas or providers.

Exit criterion: real drivers understand the winning duty and its confidence without learning the hierarchy diagram.

### Phase 8 — Adoption validation

- Repeat the blind test with new participants and all three Phase 0A personas.
- Measure time to demonstrated value, completion/abandonment, wrong defaults, trust comprehension and return-task success.
- Validate on phone first, then tablet/desktop, offline and installed PWA.

No phase should redesign schema-5 semantics merely to rearrange presentation. Any future published-roster model deserves its own reviewed data/security project.

## 15. Things not to redesign in v0.32

The following are already strong or strategically sound and should be preserved unless testing proves a specific problem:

- the configured Home combination of Today, current period and expected pay;
- preview-before-save and clear ability to change setup;
- the scan-friendly week/fortnight roster view;
- one-tap access to a day's actual-work editor;
- separation of baseline roster information from actual diary records;
- expected-pay and payslip-comparison capability;
- effective-dated roster and employment histories;
- manual patterns and ad-hoc entry as inclusive fallback capabilities;
- automatic calendar versus one-off export as a meaningful choice;
- offline/local-first operation and no mandatory account for today's product;
- schema-5 canonical integrity, generation ancestry, transactional replacement and fail-closed recovery;
- fresh capability attestation, revision conflict protection, encrypted Cloud and downgrade protection;
- controlled PWA updates, atomic caches and mobile standalone behavior accepted in v0.31;
- source transparency and honest qualification of estimates;
- backup/recovery evidence preservation rather than casual clearing;
- mobile bottom navigation and generally generous touch targets;
- the ability for a power user to inspect and correct detailed pay, allowance, leave and roster history.

Do not merge “projected,” “published” and “actual” into one mutable duty merely to simplify the screen. Simplify presentation while preserving authority and evidence.

## 16. Risks and dependencies for eventual automatic roster ingestion

### Authorisation and identity

- Weekly/daily source documents contain many employees' personal information. Browser-side bulk ingestion or public static assets are unacceptable.
- Per-user delivery needs a trustworthy association between identity and employee/departmental identifier.
- Microsoft/Entra or an employer-approved provider may help, but employer cooperation, claims quality, lifecycle and offboarding cannot be assumed.
- Identity verification and RosterBot's private diary/pay data require explicit access boundaries; organisational identity must not imply employer access to personal notes.

### Source legitimacy and timeliness

- The product needs an authorised, reliable acquisition path and evidence of source/version/publication time.
- Weekly and daily formats may change, contain corrections or be published late.
- “No new roster” must be distinguishable from an ingestion outage.
- A source can be authentic yet incomplete; RosterBot must not upgrade authority beyond what the document actually establishes.

### Matching and ambiguity

- Names, departmental numbers, depot transfers and duplicate/changed identifiers can create dangerous false matches.
- The UX needs a safe confirmation/recovery path when association is uncertain.
- A weekly shift number may not contain full duty contents; joining it to shift-book data must retain confidence and effective-date evidence.

### Provenance and history

- To report changes, the system must retain authorised prior assignments and the relationship between projection, weekly and daily facts.
- Retention must be minimised and governed; “keep everything forever” is not an acceptable default for multi-employee operational source data.
- The browser should receive only the authenticated user's necessary projection, not the underlying multi-employee document.

### Offline and reconciliation

- The latest verified per-user roster must remain useful offline without making stale data look current.
- New official data arriving after offline manual edits needs a reviewed merge policy. Actual worked records must remain authoritative; assignment corrections require clear provenance rather than last-write-wins.
- Existing Cloud revision/capability protections remain necessary but do not by themselves define multi-source roster reconciliation.

### Change semantics

- A different formatting representation is not necessarily an operational change.
- A daily confirmation matching the weekly assignment should not generate noise.
- The product must define material changes—start time, duty number/content, rest/work status, location and cancellation—and how acknowledgements behave across devices.

### Privacy, security and organisational boundaries

- Open-source client code and private operational data must remain separable.
- Service workers and Cache Storage must continue to exclude authenticated roster responses.
- Logging, analytics and support evidence must not leak names, identifiers, duties, pay or source-document contents.
- Data-controller responsibilities, consent, retention, deletion, breach response and employer policy need resolution before production ingestion.

### Product dependency

v0.32 should create language and UI seams for provenance without pretending the ingestion service exists. The safe near-term outcome is a clearer manual/local product whose Home and data-source language can accept automatic assignments later. Building speculative authentication screens, notification systems or source schemas before an authorised feed and identity contract exist would be premature.

## Final product answers

### What should a completely new user see in the first 30 seconds?

A concise promise, a believable sample of Today + next work/rest + expected pay, an explanation that it works offline and can be used locally without an account, and two honest routes: “Try a sample” and “Set up my roster manually.” Returning users should separately see “Restore my RosterBot.”

### What is the minimum information needed for first genuine value today?

For the supported master-roster path: the effective roster week/date, depot, roster and line, plus alternation only if it applies. Those inputs can show a useful roster before employment classification/history is requested. For users without a master rotation, a minimal regular pattern or a clearly manual fortnight is the fallback. Pay-specific facts should be requested when the user chooses to make the pay estimate accurate.

### What should be postponed?

Role-effective history, PDT details, annual leave, manual patterns for unaffected users, Cloud/encryption, calendar publication, backup mechanics, payslip checking and detailed allowances should appear when their benefit is visible.

### How should manual setup survive future ingestion?

As a complete, explicit fallback projection editor and correction path. It should produce the same safe canonical local state, remain offline-capable, and never be overwritten silently. Automatic sources should layer over relevant dates while the master/manual baseline stays inspectable.

### What should Home become?

The immediate, source-aware answer to today, tomorrow, material changes, next rest and expected pay. It should normally show one best-known duty, reveal provenance when it affects confidence, and route directly to actual-work correction.

### Which feature is the central promise?

The combined **Today + what changed + current fortnight + expected pay** experience. Calendar, Cloud, backups and detailed engines support that promise; they are not the promise themselves.

### Which redesign work would be wasteful?

A large visual redesign of the full current onboarding form, elaborate education about schedule modes/line parity, or making current Roster Lookup more polished without separating its jobs. Future automation should remove those burdens from normal use. The current path needs correctness, clarity and fallback quality—not elevation into the permanent product model.
