# RosterBot v0.32 Phase 8 — Adoption validation and readiness

Phase-8 baseline: `26f87a4634f36408c642d0f97c323d96ea883034`

This report began with a fresh-profile, phone-first black-box assessment. The observations in the next section were recorded before inspecting implementation source for Phase 8.

## 1. Black-box adoption verdict

v0.32 has achieved the central adoption goal. A new driver can now understand the promise from the first screen, see a realistic useful RosterBot in one tap without supplying information, reach a personal master-roster preview through familiar roster facts, and understand local storage, backup and optional sync without learning the architecture.

The least-patient reasonable user now has a credible low-risk route to value. The strongest residual friction is no longer the normal onboarding path; it is the inherent density of browsing an entire master rotation on a phone and the additional setup genuinely required by non-master work patterns.

No critical adoption blocker was found in the ordinary master-roster journey.

## 2. Fresh-user first 30 seconds

The first phone viewport communicates:

- the promise: know what you are working and what you should be paid;
- roster, diary, pay and offline availability;
- one visually dominant low-risk `Try RosterBot` action;
- an honest personal `Set up my roster` alternative;
- low-commitment roster/shift browsing;
- local-first and optional-Cloud trust facts before personal questions;
- returning-user actions behind a quiet disclosure.

A normal user can explain the product from this screen alone. The hierarchy does not imply that an account is required.

## 3. Persona journeys

### Curious driver / try before committing

One tap opens a clearly fictional sample with Today, four forward calendar weeks, OR/PH treatment, tappable duty details and expected gross pay. No personal field is requested. The sample visually belongs to the same product as the front door and contains an obvious route into real setup.

Likely demonstrated-value time: about 5–15 seconds, depending on whether the user scans Today only or also the forward roster and pay. Interaction count: one.

### Driver who wants their roster

The normal master-roster route has two oriented steps:

1. choose the recognisable work situation;
2. provide the applicable week, depot, roster and line.

It then shows a two-week preview before acceptance. Pay classification, role dates, annual leave, employment history and internal scheduling modes are absent.

Measured structure before preview:

- two visible steps/screens;
- seven interactions including entry and `Show my roster`;
- five user decisions: work situation plus four roster facts;
- four explicit values: applicable week, depot, roster and line;
- approximately three to five domain terms, chiefly ordinary railway terms (`WC`, depot, roster, line, alternation), rather than ten to fifteen mixed implementation concepts;
- one further action to accept the preview;
- exactly one intentional canonical successor on acceptance (`generation 1 → 2`).

A realistic human should normally reach preview in roughly 30–60 seconds when they know their roster details. The automated path rendered preview immediately after the seventh interaction; no artificial timing precision is claimed.

### Driver interested in pay

The fictional sample demonstrates the payoff immediately. For personal data, the accepted roster remains useful without a fabricated pay amount. Home says `Set up pay estimate`, explains why a role is required, and asks for one current role with a clear `Maybe later` exit. Accurate current-pay setup therefore adds one decision and one confirmation after roster value rather than blocking it.

### Returning driver

`Already use RosterBot?` expands to two plain routes: `Sign in & restore` and `Restore a .rosterbot backup`. Existing configured state opens directly to Home in the established regression fixtures.

### Privacy-conscious user

The front door says the working copy can stay on the device, no account is required, Cloud Sync is optional, entering details does not upload them, and offline use continues after installation.

Data & Sync reinforces a coherent model:

- working copy on this device;
- point-in-time `.rosterbot` backup;
- backup restore for a one-off move;
- optional encrypted Cloud Sync for ongoing coordination across multiple devices;
- an account is not the same as enabling Cloud Sync;
- technical details remain disclosed rather than dominating.

## 5. Configured Home validation

The accepted hierarchy remains Search → Today → next four weeks → Pay.

- Today answers date, duty, sign-on/off, roster/line and complete local shift content with zero interaction.
- The four-week Sunday–Saturday view exposes WC headings, current week, OR, PH, actual markers and individual day detail without a duplicate Tomorrow/Next-OR summary.
- Pay remains truthful when unconfigured and prominent when configured.
- Local reload reached application-ready in approximately 0.65 seconds in the controlled profile. The exact number is environment-specific.
- At 390 × 844, Today is within the first viewport and the four-week heading begins at its lower edge. Pay requires scrolling, which matches its priority.
- At 200% root text and at tablet width, the tested Home had zero horizontal document overflow.

## 6. Search, roster browsing and alternative setup paths

Search is immediately available at the top of Home. A common duty search returned a concise duty result; an unknown code produced an understandable empty state. Two minor polish candidates require source-informed confirmation: duplicated depot text in one result (`SCS · SCS`) and apparently repeated no-result wording.

`Browse rosters & shifts` is genuinely non-committal and exposes supplied master rotations and shift-book details without pay or personal setup. Its complete 16-line seven-day rotation is necessarily dense and vertically long on a phone. This is moderate utility density, not an adoption blocker because it is an explicitly selected reference task.

The regular-pattern and varying-shifts branches use recognisable user situations rather than engine names. They necessarily ask for more work-pattern information, and the varying path explicitly promises not to invent unknown shifts.

## 7. Accessibility and device observations

- front-door and sample actions had descriptive accessible names;
- calendar days exposed date, work/OR/PH state, duty and sign-on without relying on colour;
- Today exposed a concise action name rather than duplicating its entire visual card;
- setup controls had programmatic labels;
- the accepted dialog containment and focus architecture remained apparent in the tested tasks;
- 200% text produced no Home horizontal overflow;
- tablet Home remained vertically chronological.

Real-device VoiceOver has structural/automated evidence but has not been manually accepted by the product owner. v0.32 must not claim manual VoiceOver validation.

## 8. Blind-pass residual findings

1. Search may repeat its no-result message and may duplicate `SCS` in duty metadata. Confirm in source and fix only if the duplication is real.
2. The unreleased branch still presents `v0.31.0`; release metadata must be changed consistently during release-candidate preparation, not piecemeal in this UX phase.
3. The complete roster browser is information-dense on phone, although its purpose and exit are clear.
4. Local unbundled testing can surface an update-ready diagnostic when generated build files and the previously served local worker differ. This was treated as a harness condition, not production UX evidence; exact beta/release bundles must remain coherent.

No finding justifies another broad redesign.

## 9. Phase-7 accepted polish

The isolated published-roster research card now presents one `Sign on / off` fact using the compact range `08:00 → 16:00` rather than separate sign-on and sign-off boxes. Its accessible card name continues to identify the duty and sign-on, and its 200% text contract remains covered by the Phase-7 browser test. No other research presentation or source semantics changed.

The research route remains beta-only, absent from ordinary navigation, the production bundle, runtime inventory and service-worker cache. It still loads no personal persistence or Cloud modules.

## 10. Source-informed polish and terminology audit

The apparent repeated `No matching roster data found` in the black-box text transcript is intentional rather than duplicated visible presentation: one copy is the visible empty result and one is the visually hidden polite status announcement. Removing either would weaken comprehension or screen-reader feedback.

The `SCS · SCS` duty metadata was a real visible duplication caused by equal depot and shift-book labels being joined without de-duplication. Search now emits each distinct context value once while retaining both when they genuinely differ.

The user-facing `Rest` audit found no remaining use of Rest as a synonym for Off Roster in current v0.32 surfaces. Remaining occurrences are legitimate `Rest Job` pay/allowance language, historical changelog evidence, internal HTTP `rest` helpers, or local implementation variables. They were deliberately preserved.

## 11. Dead and redundant UX audit

The accepted Home surface is clean: Search, Today, four-week roster and Pay are the only visible primary hierarchy. Legacy dashboard cards and progress/history strips remain in the document but are explicitly hidden in configured v0.32 Home mode. Compatibility-only state targets are also contained in a permanently hidden compatibility block.

Removing those elements would be code cleanup rather than an adoption improvement and would create unnecessary regression risk in this readiness phase. They should be considered for a later evidence-led technical cleanup, not exposed again and not removed opportunistically before v0.32 release.

No obsolete setup CTA competes with the front door. The old Roster Lookup job has been replaced by genuine roster/shift browsing, while the configured-user roster history/change workflow remains available under Roster and More.

## 12. Published-roster future readiness

The normal app continues to use only truthful current sources such as `Roster projection`, `Saved work pattern` and `Recorded actual`. Synthetic Weekly/Daily states remain restricted to the direct Phase-7 research route.

The current UI leaves stable seams for future work:

- Today and calendar cells already render one winning duty;
- day detail already has a source/status location;
- Actual is already visually and semantically distinct;
- the Phase-7 prototype demonstrates restrained `Changed` treatment and progressively disclosed Duty history;
- Search can continue to return only the eventual winning duty;
- Pay can consume the winning expected duty while preserving Actual as the diary/pay authority.

No ingestion, source persistence, employee identity, official-document storage or notification backend was added.

## 13. Readiness assessment

v0.32 is product-ready for release-candidate preparation subject to the normal release metadata, deterministic bundle and full regression gates. The adoption work should now stop expanding: no remaining finding warrants reopening Home, onboarding, Data & Sync or accessibility information architecture.

Known validation limitations:

- product-owner real-device VoiceOver testing has not been completed;
- the full roster browser remains dense on phone by nature of the reference task;
- automatic published-roster source terminology and acknowledgement policy remain future research decisions;
- application version text still reflects the released baseline until a coordinated v0.32 release-candidate metadata update.
